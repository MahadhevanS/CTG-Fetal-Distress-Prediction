# Forensic audit of the CTU-UHB benchmark papers

Completed 2026-09-03. **Every source is archived locally in
[literature_archive/](literature_archive/)** — PDFs plus extracted plain text —
so every claim below is verifiable offline, permanently, without a working link.

| local artifact | paper |
|---|---|
| `literature_archive/dang_2026_crossformer.pdf` / `.txt` | Dang, Nguyen & Ho 2026, CTG-CrossFormer (E3S Web Conf., AIEI 2026) |
| `literature_archive/foundation_model_2601.06149.pdf` / `.txt` | Fridman & Ben Shachar 2026, foundation model (arXiv 2601.06149) |

Our own counter-evidence is equally self-contained: every number attributed to
this project below is regenerated by `scripts/audit_evaluation_protocol.py`,
which needs no network and no GPU. Its output is
[evaluation_protocol_evidence.md](evaluation_protocol_evidence.md).

## 1. Why this audit was run

An earlier draft of this project's positioning claimed that published AUROCs of
0.822–0.83 on CTU-UHB were inflated by (a) window-level data leakage and (b)
time-confounded labels. Both claims were checked against the actual papers.

**Both were wrong, and this repository already contained the refutations.**

- `src/models/train_ctg_crossformer.py` line 4 states that the script
  "Replicates the paper training protocol (Dang et al., 2026, Section 3.3):
  Stratified 5-Fold Patient-Level CV (Grouped by Patient ID)".
- [literature_preprocessing_comparison.md](literature_preprocessing_comparison.md)
  states that "the horizon rule is this project's own invention. No paper
  reviewed labels windows by position within the recording."

The critique was built out of findings that our own notes had already localised
to us. That is recorded here so it is not rediscovered and believed.

## 2. Dang et al. 2026 — CTG-CrossFormer (AUC 0.822)

### Verified clean on leakage

Section 3.3, quoted verbatim:

> "5-fold **Stratified Group K-Fold** cross-validation ensures that all windows
> from the same patient appear exclusively in either the training or validation
> set, **preventing data leakage**."

Their per-fold AUCs — 0.903, 0.802, 0.808, 0.806, 0.808 (mean 0.825) — are
stable, which is what a correctly grouped split looks like. **Do not accuse this
paper of window-level leakage.** It does not have it.

### Stated method, for the record

| aspect | value |
|---|---|
| cohort | 552 recordings → **404 patients** after excluding >50% missing FHR |
| label | pH < 7.15 → Acidosis, applied at recording level |
| windowing | 20-minute windows, **5-minute stride** → **1,753 windows** (16.6% positive) |
| interpolation | linear, gaps ≤ 15 s only; longer gaps preserved |
| split | 5-fold Stratified Group K-Fold, patient-grouped |
| AUC | "**computed on pooled predictions across all folds**" (Table 1 caption) |
| significance | DeLong's test on AUC; McNemar's on classification |
| stopping | "Early stopping with patience of 15 epochs **monitors validation AUC-ROC**" |

### Three findings that do apply — none of them leakage

**F1. Epoch selection on the reported fold.** There is no inner validation split
described and no held-out test set; the cross-validation is the whole evaluation.
As described, the fold used to select the stopping epoch is the fold being
reported. This project has already measured that bias, on this architecture and
this data — see the `--early_stop_mode` help text in
`src/models/train_ctg_crossformer.py`:

> "inflates CV AUROC by ~0.077 (measured 2026-08-19: 0.8243 reported vs 0.7472
> final-epoch vs 0.7305 held-out test)"

**0.822 − 0.077 ≈ 0.745.**

*Caveat to carry into any writeup:* if an inner split was used, the paper does
not say so. State this as "as described in the paper," not as fact about the
implementation.

**F2. DeLong's test on 1,753 pooled, overlapping windows.** 404 patients yield
1,753 windows — 4.3 per patient — and at a 5-minute stride on 20-minute windows,
adjacent windows share 75% of their samples. DeLong's test assumes independent
observations. Measured on our comparable data (`clustering` probe):

| bootstrap unit | 95% CI width |
|---|---:|
| windows (assumes independence) | 0.0311 |
| patients (respects clustering) | 0.0830 |

The independence assumption understates the interval by **2.7×**. The reported
p < 0.001 is not supportable as computed. The AUC point estimate is unaffected.

**F3. The unit of analysis is the window, never the patient.** No patient-level
figure appears anywhere in the paper, although the clinical question is
per-patient. On identical predictions we measure window 0.6159 vs patient 0.7290
(`aggregation` probe) — the two are simply not the same quantity.

### One thing that is *not* a criticism

Their 404-patient cohort comes from a **signal-quality** filter (>50% missing
FHR excluded), not from dropping borderline-pH cases. Our separate finding that
excluding pH 7.10–7.20 is worth +0.046 must **not** be attributed to them. It is
an independent sensitivity analysis. Note also that quality filtering *hurt* in
our hands (0.7290 → 0.7112 at top-75%), the opposite direction.

## 3. Fridman & Ben Shachar 2026 — foundation model (AUC 0.83)

### Verified clean on leakage

The split is at the **recording** level, and CTU-UHB recordings are individual
deliveries:

> "The dataset was split into training, validation, and test sets with
> stratified sampling to maintain consistent class distribution across splits"

| split | n | acidemia | prevalence |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Train | 441 | 90 | 20.4% |
| Validation | 56 | 12 | 21.4% |
| Test | 55 | 11 | 20.0% |

AUC is reported on the 55 held-out recordings, not on thousands of overlapping
windows. **No window leakage.** They also publish their splits (Zenodo
10.5281/zenodo.18034361), which is better reproducibility practice than most of
this literature.

### The finding that does apply: severe underpowering

**11 positive test recordings.** Our bootstrap with 17 positives gives a 95% CI
width of 0.27; with 11 it is wider still. Their 0.826 is not statistically
separable from roughly 0.68 — i.e. from the bottom of the published range. The
headline "highest reported performance on this benchmark" rests on an interval
that overlaps essentially every prior result.

### A factual inconsistency worth noting

Table 2 gives the test split as **55 recordings, 11 acidemia, 20.0%**. Table 3's
"All test cases" row gives **55, 12, 21.4%** — which are Table 2's *validation*
figures. The body text agrees with Table 2 ("11 acidemia cases, 20.0%"). Anyone
recomputing a confidence interval needs to know which is correct, since 11 vs 12
positives changes it materially.

### Not specified

The paper does not describe how the recording-level label is assigned to
individual segments during supervised fine-tuning, though it specifies 7.5-minute
overlapping windows at inference. It also concedes the instance-label problem
directly — that labelling every segment of an acidemic delivery as positive
"assumes the abnormality is continuously detectable, which is clinically
unrealistic" — and does not solve it. **That concession is the opening for this
project's MIL contribution.**

## 4. Wider literature

Record-level splitting is standard practice in recent CTG work. The npj Women's
Health CTG deep-learning paper explicitly assigns record identifiers to a
held-out set before cross-validation specifically to prevent cropped-signal
leakage.

The leakage-inflation literature is real and substantial — documented in EEG,
OCT and ECG, with reported inflation of 5–30% — **but it is not the CTG papers
this project benchmarks against.**

**Conclusion: the window-leakage negative control has no target in this
literature.** It stands as a property of the benchmark and a methodological
caution for future work, not as a criticism of anyone's published result.

## 5. What this project can defensibly claim

Not an audit. A **reconciliation**.

> Published CTU-UHB performance (0.822–0.83) and honest patient-level
> performance (~0.73–0.78) differ by roughly 0.08, and that gap is accounted for
> by three specific, measurable methodological choices — epoch selection on the
> reported fold, window-level rather than patient-level analysis units, and
> uncertainty computed under an independence assumption that is understated
> 2.7×. Once corrected, the literature and this work agree.

Supporting claims, each backed by a probe in
`scripts/audit_evaluation_protocol.py`:

1. **Negative control.** Window-random splitting of overlapping CTG windows
   yields 0.9112 AUROC on labels containing no clinical information. The
   mechanism is patient re-identification: a held-out window's nearest neighbour
   is from the same labour 74.6% of the time, 423× chance. Benchmark property,
   not accusation.
2. **Label construction.** Labels defined relative to end-of-recording are 88%
   predictable from elapsed time alone and are uncomputable at inference. This
   was *this project's* bug and is reported as such.
3. **Honest baseline.** 0.7290 ± 0.045 patient-level over 547 patients;
   0.7751 ± 0.073 on a declared 431-patient cohort with borderline pH excluded.
4. **Methodological contribution.** MIL addresses the instance-labelling problem
   the foundation-model paper concedes and leaves open.

## 6. What still needs checking

- Whether Dang et al.'s released implementation matches the paper's description,
  particularly whether an inner validation split exists. The paper says the right
  thing; the code is what matters.
- Whether the foundation model's Table 2 or Table 3 test-set composition is
  correct.
- The older CTU-UHB literature (Petrozziello, Ogasawara, Fergus, M'Barek /
  DeepCTG) has not been audited to this depth. If the window-leakage control has
  a real target anywhere, it is more likely there.

## 7. Reconciliation: what actually explains 0.822 vs 0.7271

Added 2026-09-04. Every number below is measured, by
[`scripts/probe_prior_art_cohort.py`](../scripts/probe_prior_art_cohort.py), on
the frozen protocol.

### 7.1 Two arguments that are NOT available

Before listing what can be claimed, two tempting arguments must be ruled out,
because the evidence contradicts them.

**(a) "We solve a different, prospective task."** We do not. Dang et al. derive
binary labels from umbilical-artery pH at the **recording** level (pH < 7.15) and
apply them to 20-minute windows. That is exactly what `pipeline_clinical.py`
does. Neither paper uses a prediction horizon —
[literature_preprocessing_comparison.md](literature_preprocessing_comparison.md)
already established that the horizon rule was **this project's own invention**,
and it has since been removed. The endpoint, the dataset and the observation
unit are the same. **A task-definition or prediction-horizon defence is not
supportable and must not be used.**

**(b) "Their window-level metric inflates the number."** Measured on identical
out-of-fold predictions, the effect runs the **opposite** way:

| unit | AUROC, all 547 |
|---|---:|
| window-level pooled (their metric) | 0.6179 |
| **patient-level, max-aggregated (ours)** | **0.7271** |

Patient-level scores **+0.1092 higher**, not lower. Moving Dang et al. to a
patient-level metric would, on this evidence, be expected to *raise* their
figure. The unit difference is a genuine incomparability, but it does not favour
us and cannot be presented as a source of inflation.

### 7.2 The cohort discrepancy — verified

The paper states: *"After quality filtering (recordings with >50% missing FHR
values are excluded), 404 patients remain."*

Measured directly over all 552 records:

| statistic | FHR missing ratio |
|---|---:|
| median | 0.183 |
| 95th percentile | 0.414 |
| **maximum** | **0.535** |

| threshold | records removed | remaining |
|---|---:|---:|
| **>50 % (as stated)** | **5** | **547** |
| >30 % | 105 | 447 |
| >25 % | 163 | 389 |

> **A >50 % missing-FHR rule removes five records and leaves 547 — exactly this
> project's cohort. It cannot leave 404.** To leave 404 the threshold would have
> to be ≈26 %.

The paper's stated criterion is arithmetically inconsistent with its stated
cohort size by a factor of ~30 in records excluded. The paper does not specify
how "missing" was measured, so the discrepancy is **unexplained, not
attributable** — state it as an inconsistency, never as misconduct.

What it means practically: their analysis cohort is the cleanest ~73 % of
CTU-UHB by signal quality, however it was actually selected.

### 7.3 What the cohort restriction is worth

Restricting to the 404 cleanest records (missing ratio ≤ 0.261), same model,
same frozen folds:

| | window-level | patient-level |
|---|---:|---:|
| all 547 | 0.6179 | 0.7271 |
| **cleanest 404** | **0.6507** | **0.7530** |

Cohort effect: **+0.0328** at window level, **+0.0259** at patient level.
Prevalence falls from 20.1 % to 18.8 %.

Real, but modest. **It does not explain a 0.20 gap.**

### 7.4 The honest decomposition

| component | measured | status |
|---|---:|---|
| cohort restriction to the cleanest 404 | +0.033 | verified here |
| epoch selection on the reported fold | ≈ +0.077 | measured on this repo's CrossFormer, §2 F1 |
| window vs patient unit | −0.109 (**against us**) | verified here |
| DeLong on overlapping windows | CI understated 2.7× | affects *p*-values, not the point estimate |
| **remainder** | **unexplained** | see 7.5 |

### 7.5 The reproduction was attempted and did not succeed

[model8_crossformer_run_history.md](model8_crossformer_run_history.md) records a
literal reproduction attempt via `pipeline_paper_match.py` +
`scripts/pooled_oof_ctg_crossformer.py`, identifying eight protocol differences.
Outcome: **folds 0.6193 / 0.7039 — well below 0.822.** The run was *"stopped
early at user request; paper reproduction abandoned."*

This is the weakest point in the project's position and must be stated plainly:

> **We have not reproduced 0.822, and we have not fully explained it.** The
> cohort inconsistency and the epoch-selection bias are documented and
> quantified; a residual gap remains, and the experiment that would resolve it
> was abandoned rather than completed.

Note also that the comparison in 7.3 uses a **19-feature logistic regression**,
not a CrossFormer. Comparing it to Dang et al.'s deep model is not like-for-like
and should not be presented as one.

### 7.6 The foundation model needs a different argument entirely

Fridman & Ben Shachar report 0.83 on **55 held-out recordings, 11 positive** —
already patient-level, so none of the above applies. Hanley–McNeil:

| | AUROC | 95 % CI |
|---|---:|---|
| Fridman & Ben Shachar (11 positives) | 0.83 | **0.673 – 0.987** |
| this project (110 positives) | 0.727 | 0.670 – 0.785 |

**The intervals overlap almost completely.** The correct statement is that the
two results are *statistically indistinguishable*, not that either is better.
This is a power argument, and it is strong precisely because it makes no claim
about their method.

### 7.7 What can therefore be written

Supportable:

1. The prior-art figure is **window-level pooled** and the present work is
   **patient-level**; these are different quantities. (State it as
   incomparability, and disclose that the direction favours the prior art.)
2. Dang et al.'s stated exclusion criterion is **arithmetically inconsistent**
   with their stated cohort size, verified independently; their effective cohort
   is the cleanest ~73 % of the database, worth ~+0.03 here.
3. The reported figure is obtained with **epoch selection on the reported fold**
   as described, worth ≈ +0.077 on this architecture and data.
4. The 0.83 foundation-model result rests on **11 positive test recordings** and
   is statistically indistinguishable from 0.727.
5. Under a frozen, pre-registered, patient-level protocol this dataset yields
   ~0.73, and **eight independent information sources fail to improve it**
   ([phase8_information_audit.md](phase8_information_audit.md)).

Not supportable:

- that this project solves a different or more prospective task;
- that window-level evaluation inflates the prior-art number;
- that the prior-art results are "wrong" — the reproduction was abandoned, so no
  such claim has been earned.
