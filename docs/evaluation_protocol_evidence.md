# Evaluation-protocol evidence

Generated by `scripts/audit_evaluation_protocol.py`. No network, no GPU -- reproducible offline from `data/processed_clinical/`.

Cohort: 8517 windows | 547 patients | 110 positive (20.1%) | 15.6 windows per patient

> The `protocol` probe is a negative control describing what this benchmark permits. It is NOT an accusation about any published paper -- Dang et al. report patient-grouped folds and the 2026 foundation model splits at recording level. See the module docstring.

## protocol -- the 2x2 negative control

Identical data, features and classifier throughout. Only the split rule and the labels change.

| model | labels | patient-grouped | window-random | inflation |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| LogReg | real | 0.6144 | 0.6493 | +0.0350 |
| LogReg | RANDOM | 0.4735 | 0.5595 | +0.0860 |
| RandomForest | real | 0.6254 | 0.8979 | +0.2725 |
| RandomForest | RANDOM | 0.4878 | 0.9112 | +0.4235 |

A window-random split scores far above chance on RANDOM labels. Low-capacity models barely benefit; high-capacity models benefit enormously -- which is the signature of memorisation, not of a weak signal being amplified.

## identity -- the mechanism

8517 windows, 547 patients. Chance that a random other window shares your patient: 0.0018

| retrieval | rate | vs chance |
|---|---:|---:|
| nearest neighbour is same patient | 0.7462 | 423x |
| same patient within top-5 | 0.8579 | 97x |

Windows of one labour cluster tightly. Under a window-random split those clusters straddle train and test, so the model retrieves the patient and the label comes along for free.

## label -- reconstructing this project's OLD horizon rule

patient label positives: 1659 (19.5% of windows) | horizon label positives: 438 (5.1%)

| label | window AUROC (grouped) | AUROC from ELAPSED TIME alone |
|---|---:|---:|
| patient (clean, current) | 0.6144 | 0.4920 |
| horizon (old pipeline) | 0.8087 | 0.8802 |

A clock with no access to the heart-rate signal beats a trained model on the horizon label, and scores chance on the clean one. The confound lived entirely in the label. That rule was this project's own; no reviewed paper uses it.

## clustering -- what an independence assumption costs

Out-of-fold window AUROC (patient-grouped): 0.6174

| bootstrap unit | 95% CI | width |
|---|---|---:|
| windows (assumes independence) | [0.6022, 0.6333] | 0.0311 |
| patients (respects clustering) | [0.5752, 0.6581] | 0.0830 |

Treating overlapping windows as independent understates the interval by 2.7x. Any p-value computed under that assumption -- DeLong's included -- is overstated by a corresponding amount.
